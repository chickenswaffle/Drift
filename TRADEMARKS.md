The **code** in this repository is MIT-licensed (see [`LICENSE`](LICENSE)) —
use it, fork it, modify it, ship it, sell it. That is deliberate and permanent.

The **names are not part of that license.** "DRIFT", "Drift Protocol",
"DRIFT-P", the DRIFT wordmark, and the project logo identify *this* project
and its stewards. The MIT license grants rights to the software; it grants no
rights to these marks.

What this means in practice:

- **You may** say your software *"implements the Drift Protocol (DRIFT-P/1)"*
  or *"is derived from DRIFT"* — truthful, descriptive references are always
  fine.
- **You may not** name a fork, product, relay service, or company "DRIFT" or
  "Drift Protocol", use the wordmark/logo, or otherwise present your
  distribution as the official project without written permission.
- **Compatibility claims** should be verifiable: if you claim DRIFT-P/1
  compatibility, your implementation should pass the public conformance
  checks (see `scripts/gauntlet.py` and the spec in
  [`PROTOCOL.md`](PROTOCOL.md)).

Why: anyone can carry the code — that is the point of releasing it MIT. What
users must be able to trust is that something *calling itself* DRIFT is the
audited-lineage, spec-faithful project and not an impostor with a familiar
name. The mark is how they can tell.

requests: open an issue or use the security contact in
[`SECURITY.md`](SECURITY.md).
